  • Luther Vandross (born April 20, 1951, New York, New York, U.S.—died July 1, 2005, Edison, New Jersey) was an American soul and pop singer, songwriter, and producer whose widespread popularity and reputation as a consummate stylist made him a leading recording artist beginning in the early 1980s.
  • He also contributed vocals to David Bowie’s 1975 album Young Americans and received a writing credit on the track “Fascination.” Among the other artists he provided background vocals for were Bette Midler, Quincy Jones, Carly Simon, and Roberta Flack.
  • Breakthrough and success As a featured vocalist on the album The Glow of Love (1980) by the disco group Change, Vandross came to the attention of record executives and signed with Epic, which allowed him to write and produce his own material.
  • In 1981 Vandross’s first album for that label, Never Too Much, sold more than one million copies, and its title song was a number-one rhythm-and-blues (R&B) hit.
  • So began a long string of million-selling albums that featured Vandross’s distinctive baritone, precise phrasing, and unabashedly romantic songs, including “Here and Now,” for which he won his first Grammy Award in 1990.
  • Britannica Quiz Pop Culture Quiz Vandross’s 1991 album Power of Love and the eponymous song “Power of Love/Love Power” both received Grammys, as did the song “Your Secret Love” (1996).
  • As a producer, Vandross was responsible for successful albums for Dionne Warwick and Aretha Franklin, who, along with the Supremes, influenced his music.
  • Dance with My Father and death In 2003 Vandross suffered a debilitating stroke shortly before the release of what proved to be his last studio album, Dance with My Father, which earned four Grammy Awards, including best R&B album, while the title track took the award for song of the year.
  • That same year the song “Luther” by rapper Kendrick Lamar and SZA—which is named for Vandross and features a sample of his 1982 duet with Cheryl Lynn, “If This World Were Mine”—won the Grammy for record of the year.

  • In 1996 she signed with Atlantic Records.
  • After releasing a holiday album (2005), she focused on performing.
